# DashChat — Product Notes

## One-liner

A smart way to talk to your dashboards.

## Who it's for

DashChat is for product and platform teams who want to add a dependable natural-language and voice layer to an existing app — analytics tools, CRMs, billing consoles, admin panels — without surrendering control to a browser agent. Developers register typed capabilities with the SDK; product and design teams use the Northgrid demo and the capability console to explore, test, and operate those capabilities through familiar workflows.

## What it does

DashChat turns a product's defined routes, actions, schemas, reads, and confirmation policies into a reusable capability runtime that any typed or spoken request can safely drive. Success means a voice command and a mouse click land on the *same* guarded handler — no duplicated logic, no pixel-scraping, no silent side effects.

## Brand personality

Technical, precise, and calm. Engineered rather than theatrical: direct language, clear hierarchy, restrained motion, and a single electric-lime signal used only where an action or a state change genuinely deserves attention.

## With DashChat, a user can…

- Ask for the metric instead of hunting for the filter.
- Draft, send, and reconcile invoices conversationally.
- Move an account between plans with a reviewed, confirmed step.
- Clear an approval queue by voice during a standup.
- Export exactly the slice they're looking at — after previewing it.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic SaaS card grids, over-rounded chrome, decorative borders, filler copy, cramped layouts, glass effects, disconnected "AI" sidebars that don't share real product logic, and chat windows that appear where a normal form or button is the right control.

## Design principles

1. Show the capability model through working product behavior, not slideware.
2. Keep voice distinct as an input, but route it through the same execution path as a click.
3. Prefer familiar application controls for manual work.
4. Use spacing and hierarchy before visual chrome.
5. Make safety and confirmation explicit without slowing routine reads.

## Accessibility & inclusion

Readable contrast, keyboard-operable controls, visible focus states, semantic dialogs and forms, responsive layouts, and reduced-motion alternatives. Never communicate status through color alone.
